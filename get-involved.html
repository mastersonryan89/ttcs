Volunteer

Bring the skill you already have

The most useful volunteers are not the ones who know reentry. They are the ones who know something concrete — how a budget works, how an interview goes, how to fix a résumé — and are willing to show up on a schedule.

Consistency matters more than hours. A person who comes every other Tuesday for six months is worth more to a participant than a large group that comes once.

Ask about volunteering
Photo needed A volunteer working one-on-one with a participant at a table, papers between them

Teach a life skills class

Financial literacy, communication, conflict resolution, goal setting or time management. Bring the subject; we handle the room and the participants.

Mentor one-on-one

Meet with the same participant on a regular schedule and be a steady presence while they rebuild. Training and boundaries are covered before you start.

Run mock interviews

Sit on the other side of the table so the first real interview is not the first one. Especially valuable if you have hired people before.

Review résumés

Work through résumés and applications with participants in our workforce development program, including how to speak about a record honestly.

Lend a professional skill

Accounting, legal, IT, design, grant writing. Small organizations run on donated expertise, and a few hours from the right person goes a long way.

Help at events and drives

Setup, sorting donations, staffing a table, cooking, cleanup. Good for groups, and a straightforward first way to see the work up close.

How it starts

What happens after you reach out

No application to fill out and no waiting to hear back. It starts with a conversation.

Step one

You call or email

Tell us what you would like to do and roughly when you are free. If you are not sure what you would be good at, say that instead — that is a normal place to start.

Step two

We sit down together

A short conversation at our High Street office about the work, what you are walking into and what would suit you. Usually under half an hour.

Step three

Orientation

What to expect from participants, where the boundaries are and why confidentiality is not optional. Short, practical, and it protects everybody.

Step four

You get matched

We place you with a program and a schedule that fits, and you have a staff contact from then on. If it turns out not to fit, we find you something else.

Partner with us

Organizations that make the whole thing work

For county agencies, treatment providers, faith communities, civic groups, schools and other nonprofits working the same streets we do.

Refer the people you cannot serve

Case managers, probation officers and discharge planners send us clients whose needs cross employment, housing and recovery at the same time. Call and describe the situation; we will tell you honestly whether we are the right fit.

Take the referrals we cannot serve

Partnership runs both directions. When someone needs specialized treatment, legal help or a service outside our scope, we want somewhere real to send them.

Bring a service to our space

Hold a clinic, a benefits enrollment session, a legal aid hour or a licensing workshop at our office. Meeting people where they already come removes the transportation barrier that kills most appointments.

Sponsor or fund a program

Businesses, congregations and foundations can underwrite a specific program rather than giving into a general fund. You will know what your money paid for.

Open your congregation or membership

Faith communities and civic groups reach volunteers, employers and landlords we cannot reach on our own. Introductions are worth as much as donations.

Collaborate on funding

We are open to joint applications and shared-outcome grants with partners whose programs complement ours. Reach out early in the cycle, not the week before.

For employers

Fill the position, keep the hire

Second Chance Staffing connects you with pre-screened, motivated candidates who have been through job readiness training before they reach your interview.

Support does not stop at the offer letter. Hiring incentives and retention support come with the placement, so when something comes up in the first few months you have someone to call who already knows the person.

Looking for help yourself?

You are on the wrong page, and that is easy to fix

This page is for people who want to support the work. If you or someone in your family needs housing, work or support in recovery, we have a page written for you.

There is no form to fill out. Call 614-502-0396 or come to 1511 S. High St between 10am and 6pm, Monday to Friday, and you will talk to a person.

Looking for services?

Family and friends

If you are trying to help someone else, you are welcome to call and ask questions on their behalf. We will tell you what we can and what the person needs to do themselves.

What we cannot do is enroll someone who has not decided to come in. That first step has to be theirs.
